# Lifestyle-Behaviors
Project Overview

This study uses the NHANES 2013–2014 cycle to examine how physical activity, sleep duration, and diet quality vary across adulthood and how these health behaviors are associated with self-reported health. Six NHANES public-use components were linked using the respondent identifier (SEQN), producing an analytic sample of 5,083 U.S. adults aged 18 years and older.

The analysis was designed around a lifespan health perspective, comparing adults across four age groups: 18–29, 30–49, 50–64, and 65+ years.

Research Objectives

The study aimed to:

Examine differences in physical activity, sleep duration, diet quality, and self-reported health across adult age groups.
Assess whether physical activity, sleep, and diet quality are independently associated with better self-reported health.
Determine whether the association between physical activity and self-reported health varies across age groups.
Key Variables
Physical activity: Standardized as MET-minutes/week using vigorous and moderate activity measures.
Sleep: Categorized according to reported weekday/workday sleep duration.
Diet quality: A study-specific nutrient-density score based on fiber, potassium, calcium, saturated fat, added sugar, and sodium.
Self-reported health: Dichotomized into good health versus fair/poor health.
Covariates: Age group, sex, BMI, poverty-income ratio, and education.
Statistical Analysis

The analysis incorporated the NHANES MEC examination weight (WTMEC2YR) to account for the complex probability sampling design. Weighted descriptive statistics and group comparisons were followed by weighted logistic regression to estimate associations between health behaviors and self-reported health.

An interaction between physical activity and age group was additionally tested to assess whether the physical activity–health association differed across the adult lifespan.

Key Findings

Physical activity declined substantially with increasing age, while nutrient-density diet quality improved among older adults. Sleep duration remained relatively stable across age groups, whereas the proportion reporting good or better health declined with age.

In the adjusted regression model, moderate physical activity was associated with higher odds of good self-rated health (OR ≈ 1.8), while short sleep (<6 hours) was associated with lower odds of good self-rated health (OR ≈ 0.57). Diet quality was also positively associated with self-reported health.

The physical activity × age-group interaction was not statistically significant (p ≈ 0.62), indicating no detectable difference in the physical activity–health association across the adult age groups in this cross-sectional sample.

Significance

The findings highlight how health behaviors change across adulthood and demonstrate the importance of considering physical activity, sleep, and diet together when studying health and well-being across the lifespan. The project also demonstrates the application of population health data, survey weighting, epidemiological analysis, and multivariable statistical modeling to investigate health patterns across different stages of adulthood.

Data Source

National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), 2013–2014, conducted by the U.S. National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS).

This analysis is based on publicly available NHANES data. The study-specific Diet Quality Score should not be interpreted as a validated external dietary index such as HEI-2015.
